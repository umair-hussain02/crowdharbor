# CrowdHarbor About Page Design Direction

## Page Goal

The About page should explain who CrowdHarbor is, why it exists, what it helps founders do, and why founders can trust the process before submitting sensitive business information.

This page should not feel like a generic “about us” page.

It should feel like a clear positioning page that explains CrowdHarbor’s mission:

**Helping founders prepare before they approach capital.**

The About page should answer:

* What is CrowdHarbor?
* Why does CrowdHarbor exist?
* Who is it for?
* What problem does it solve?
* What does CrowdHarbor believe about fundraising?
* How does CrowdHarbor help founders?
* What does CrowdHarbor not do?
* Why should a founder trust CrowdHarbor before submitting company information?

The visitor should leave thinking:

“CrowdHarbor understands the funding preparation problem, and this feels like a serious company built to help founders make better funding decisions.”

# 3. The Problem We Saw Section

## Purpose

Explain the market problem CrowdHarbor is solving.

This section should create emotional and strategic relevance.

## Section Heading

**Founders are often told to raise before they are ready.**

## Supporting Copy

Many founders are encouraged to pitch investors, launch crowdfunding campaigns, apply to accelerators, or chase grants without first understanding whether their materials, numbers, story, and pathway are ready for serious review.

## Problem Cards

Use 6 cards:

### Unclear pitch

The business may be promising, but the story is hard to understand.

### Weak financial logic

The funding ask may not clearly connect to revenue, runway, hiring, or growth assumptions.

### Missing documents

Important materials may be incomplete, unorganized, or not ready for review.

### Wrong capital path

The founder may be chasing investors when grants, crowdfunding, accelerators, or another pathway may fit better.

### Weak traction story

The company may have proof, but it is not communicated clearly.

### No preparation roadmap

The founder may know they need funding but not know what to fix first.

## Layout Direction

Use a 3-column grid on desktop.

Mobile should stack cards.

## Visual Style

* White cards
* Thin border
* Small orange warning or insight icon
* Short copy
* No harsh red
* Keep the tone strategic, not negative

## Animation

* Cards reveal in stagger
* Hover border turns orange
* Card lifts slightly

# 4. What CrowdHarbor Does Section

## Purpose

Explain the actual service clearly.

## Section Heading

**What CrowdHarbor helps founders do.**

## Supporting Copy

CrowdHarbor gives founders a structured way to understand their readiness, review important fundraising materials, identify blockers, and choose a preparation path before outreach.

## Service Pillars

Use 5 large cards:

### 1. Diagnose funding readiness

Understand where your company currently stands before approaching capital.

### 2. Review key materials

Evaluate pitch decks, financials, traction proof, use of funds, and data room readiness.

### 3. Identify blockers

See what could create confusion, hesitation, or rejection during funding review.

### 4. Recommend a capital pathway

Understand whether crowdfunding, angels, grants, accelerators, pre-seed, revenue-based financing, or another route may fit better.

### 5. Build an action plan

Know what to fix first, what to prepare next, and what to avoid before outreach.

## Layout Direction

Use a clean framework layout.

Recommended:

* One large central card: **Funding Preparation System**
* Five surrounding pillar cards

Alternative:

* 5-card horizontal/vertical grid

## Design Style

* White cards
* Orange step numbers
* Simple icons
* Clean hierarchy
* Premium advisory feel

## Animation

* Central card appears first
* Pillar cards reveal around it
* Orange connecting lines draw softly

### Not a funding guarantee

CrowdHarbor does not promise investment, grants, accelerator acceptance, crowdfunding success, or business results.

CrowdHarbor does not act as an investor, broker, dealer, crowdfunding platform, or fundraising guarantor.

CrowdHarbor focuses on preparation before outreach, not guaranteed introductions.

Use calm white cards.

* White cards

# Accessibility Direction

The About page must be easy to read and navigate.

Rules:

* Use proper H1, H2, and H3 hierarchy
* Keep body text at least 16px
* Maintain strong color contrast
* Use visible focus states for links and buttons
* Do not rely only on color for meaning
* Use icons plus labels
* Avoid tiny muted text
* Respect reduced-motion preferences
* Keep line height comfortable
